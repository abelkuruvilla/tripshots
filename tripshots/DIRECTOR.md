# Director's notes

How a TripShots reel should be directed. The critic's AI review reads these (with the style's own `direction`), so
keep them short and checkable. The pattern (shared notes for the craft, plus a short note per style) comes from the
Lemo-Opuscar skill's DIRECTOR.md (lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar, MIT).

## Story
- The first second decides. Open on the strongest picture with movement or a person, never an establishing shot.
- One idea per reel: a place, a day, a feeling. Every shot should belong to it.
- Build, then pay off: shots get shorter into the drop, the best shot lands on it and is held.
- End on a calm, wide, open shot that can loop back to the first.

## Rhythm
- Cut on the beat, but vary the lengths. A metronome is boring; long-short-short-long breathes.
- Hold a shot as long as it keeps giving: slow scenic shots 2-4 s, action 0.5-1 s.
- Most cuts are plain cuts. A transition marks a moment (a change of place, the drop). Two in a row is noise.

## Camera
- Every move has a reason: push in on a person or a detail, pan along a horizon in the direction it leads.
- Moves are slow and settle. Nothing drifts off the subject; no move fights the camera's own motion.
- Cut from movement to movement in the same direction (left to left), or from still to still.

## Light and colour
- One look for the whole reel: shots from different light must be pulled into the same mood.
- Keep skies blue (or the sunset warm), skin natural, blacks black.
- Don't put a harsh midday shot next to golden hour unless the contrast is the point.

## Text
- Few words, big enough to read on a phone, on screen long enough to read twice.
- Text sits away from faces and the subject, inside the area Instagram's buttons don't cover.
- Words arrive with the music (on a beat or a phrase), never in the middle of a cut.

## Sound
- The music leads; effects only where the picture does something (a whip, a freeze, a title).
- Quiet is allowed: a calm shot with its own wind or waves is better than another whoosh.

## Self-check before render
- Would a stranger stop on frame one? Is there a person or movement in the first 3 s?
- Does the hook line describe what is on screen while it shows?
- Does the pace match the style's own references (its `attention` profile), not a general idea of "fast"?
- Is any moment shown twice? Is anything blurry, dark or tilted?
- Does the payoff land on the drop and get held?
- Can every line of text be read before it goes?
- Does the last shot flow back into the first?
